# Compressor series — narration scripts

Written by the operator. These are the source of truth: the narration is
recorded from these, and the shot lengths are then derived from the recorded
take with `beats.mjs`. The picture is cut to the voice, never the reverse.

Each Short opens with a hook line, then the body. The hook exists so the Short
reads as native to the feed rather than as a clipped section of a longer lesson.

Style version: `ALT-HVAC-CUTAWAY-V1`.

---

## 1. Reciprocating — PUBLISHED (`DZIq2jmX_rA`, private)

> This is a reciprocating compressor — it uses a piston, a suction valve, and a
> discharge valve to compress refrigerant vapor. As the piston moves down,
> cylinder pressure drops below suction pressure, opening the suction valve and
> drawing low-pressure, low-temperature refrigerant vapor into the cylinder. As
> the piston moves back up, both valves close and the trapped vapor is
> compressed into a smaller volume. That raises both the pressure and the
> temperature of the refrigerant. Once cylinder pressure rises above discharge
> pressure, the discharge valve opens and the hot, high-pressure vapor is forced
> out toward the condenser. It's the same refrigerant — the compressor has
> simply added mechanical work, increasing its pressure and temperature.

Six sentences, six shots, 57.7 s. No hook line — it predates the hook pattern.

## 2. Scroll

**Hook:** Here's how a scroll compressor actually squeezes refrigerant.

> This is a scroll compressor. It uses two interleaved spiral scrolls — one
> fixed and one orbiting — to compress refrigerant vapor. Low-pressure vapor
> enters around the outside and gets trapped in crescent-shaped pockets. As the
> orbiting scroll moves, those pockets travel inward and get progressively
> smaller, which raises the refrigerant's pressure and temperature. At the
> center, the hot, high-pressure vapor exits through the discharge port and
> heads toward the condenser.

**Shot plan — 5 shots.** "This is a scroll compressor." is only five words, so
it shares a shot with the sentence that names the parts rather than becoming a
one-second cut.

1. Hook — whole scroll running, seen from above
2. Naming — the fixed and orbiting scrolls labelled
3. Trapping — cool vapor enters the periphery, pockets seal
4. Inward + shrinking — pockets migrate, gauges sweep
5. Discharge — centre port, hot vapor leaves

## 3. Rotary

**Hook:** This compressor uses a roller instead of a piston.

> This is a rotary compressor. Inside the cylinder, an eccentric roller and
> sliding vane create chambers that constantly change size. Low-pressure
> refrigerant vapor enters the expanding side, while vapor trapped on the other
> side is squeezed into a smaller volume. As that volume decreases, pressure and
> temperature rise. The hot, high-pressure vapor then exits through the
> discharge port and moves toward the condenser.

**Shot plan — 5 shots.**

1. Hook — roller orbiting inside the cylinder
2. Naming — eccentric roller and sliding vane labelled
3. Two chambers at once — one growing, one shrinking
4. Pressure and temperature rise
5. Discharge

The hard part visually: both chambers change at the same time, so the frame has
to carry two simultaneous states without becoming unreadable. Colour does that
work — cool on the expanding side, warming on the shrinking side.

---

## 4. Screw

**Hook:** This is how a screw compressor raises refrigerant pressure.

> This is a screw compressor. It uses two intermeshing helical rotors to
> compress refrigerant vapor. Low-pressure vapor enters at the suction end and
> becomes trapped between the rotor lobes and the housing. As the rotors turn,
> that trapped vapor moves down the length of the compressor while its volume
> gets smaller. That raises the refrigerant's pressure and temperature before it
> exits through the discharge port toward the condenser.

**Shot plan — 5 shots.**

1. Hook — rotors turning, seen along the axis
2. Naming — male and female rotors labelled
3. Trapping at the suction end
4. Axial travel + shrinking volume
5. Discharge end

The only mechanism in the series whose gas moves ALONG the machine rather than
around or across it, so the camera should travel with it.

---

## 5. Centrifugal

**Hook:** This compressor doesn't trap and squeeze refrigerant at all.

> This is a centrifugal compressor, and it works differently from the others.
> Low-pressure refrigerant vapor enters the center of a high-speed impeller. The
> impeller accelerates the vapor outward, adding energy as velocity. That
> high-speed vapor then enters the diffuser, where velocity is reduced and
> converted into static pressure. The result is high-pressure refrigerant vapor
> leaving the compressor and heading toward the condenser.

**Shot plan — 5 shots.**

1. Hook — impeller spinning
2. Naming — impeller eye, vanes, diffuser
3. Acceleration — vapor flung outward, VELOCITY rising
4. Diffuser — velocity falls, pressure rises
5. Discharge

**This one breaks the series' instrument language on purpose.** Every other
compressor raises pressure by reducing volume, so a pressure gauge tracks the
story. A centrifugal adds energy as velocity first, and pressure only appears in
the diffuser. So this Short needs a VELOCITY readout alongside the pressure
gauge, and the pressure needle must stay low through the impeller and rise in
the diffuser. Showing pressure climbing inside the impeller would be the single
biggest accuracy error available in this series.
