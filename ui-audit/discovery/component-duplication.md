## Summary
Altair OS has a real, well-documented primitive layer whose adoption is bimodal: the shell/layout tier is a success (MasterListPageLayout on 12 list pages, MasterDetailPageLayout on 5 detail pages, AltairTable on 7 of the 9 core ledgers), while the element tier is largely bypassed — DS Button reaches ~24 files against 217 shared-component files with unstyled raw buttons, Field/Input/Select/Textarea have exactly one consumer, DS EmptyState has 6 importers against 12 divergent domain empty states, and StatusPill is adapted by only 1 of ~20 domain badge components. The single largest cause is not per-page sloppiness but the NEXT_PUBLIC_NORTH_STAR_SHELL fork: 712 `northStar ?` ternaries across 160 files keep two complete visual systems alive in the same components, doubling every other duplication and letting the off-flag branch drift unreviewed. The most user-visible symptoms are three simultaneous 'primary button' looks on the billing surfaces, a lead-status palette speaking raw sky/violet/indigo against the semantic tone system, four empty-state visual families across adjacent nav items, and native window.confirm on customer/job destructive actions while invoices get a branded dialog. Highest-leverage sequence: retire the losing north-star branch, route the five status-action components through buttonClassName, and make all entity badges StatusPill adapters.

## Findings

### [P1/SYSTEMIC] Every surface carries two complete visual systems behind the northStar flag (712 ternaries, 160 files)
- category: duplication-root-cause | effort: HIGH
- evidence: grep 'northStar\s*\?' = 712 occurrences across 160 files in shared/components; isNorthStarShellEnabled referenced in 46 files (lib/beta/north-star-shell.ts:9-14, driven by NEXT_PUBLIC_NORTH_STAR_SHELL). docs/product/ALTair_MASTER_STATUS.md:195 says 'not yet default-on' while .env.local:7 sets it true. Examples of the fork living inside single components: shared/components/leads/LeadForm.tsx:90 (northStar ? ls.formInput : LEGACY_INPUT_CLASS), shared/components/leads/LeadStatusBadge.tsx:12-14 (two badge palettes), shared/components/dispatch/DispatchStatusBadge.tsx:37-39, shared/components/invoices/InvoicesEmptyState.tsx vs CustomersEmptyState.tsx, 10 of the *SearchFilterBar files (e.g. shared/components/estimates/EstimateSearchFilterBar.tsx:46).
- why: This is the single shared cause behind most duplication in this audit: every badge map, input class, empty state, and loading state exists twice, and each new feature must be styled twice. Whichever branch is not shipping drifts unreviewed yet still renders whenever the env flag is absent (fresh deploys, previews), so users can see an entire stale UI. It also doubles bundle CSS/JSX and makes every consolidation below a 2x job until the fork is retired.
- suggestion: Decide the flag's fate (status doc implies North Star is the product), flip the default, then delete the losing branch file-family by file-family. Every other finding in this report gets ~50% cheaper after this.

### [P1/SYSTEMIC] Three live 'primary button' looks; DS Button reaches only ~24 files vs 217 raw-button files
- category: primitive-adoption | effort: MEDIUM
- evidence: Adoption: 24 files render <Button> (shared/design-system/components/Button.tsx), 39 files use buttonClassName/button-styles adapters. Ad hoc: 699 raw '<button' occurrences in 264 shared/components files; 217 of those files import no DS button styling at all (176 after excluding concept/legacy dirs). Competing primary looks on ONE production page (invoice detail, both legacy and north-star paths): DS graphite primary in shared/components/invoices/InvoiceLifecycleControl.tsx:183,236 vs 'bg-slate-900 text-white hover:bg-slate-800' in InvoiceStatusActions.tsx:272, InvoiceDetailActionBar.tsx:253, InvoicePaymentCollectionCard.tsx:192 and shared/components/estimates/EstimateStatusActions.tsx:63,180,192 vs 'bg-emerald-600 text-white hover:bg-emerald-700' money CTAs in RecordPaymentForm.tsx:70,306, InvoicePaymentCollectionCard.tsx:191, InvoiceDetailActionBar.tsx:250, EstimateStatusActions.tsx:80,150. Counts: bg-slate-900 x59 (42 files), bg-emerald-600 x19.
- why: The primary action look is the strongest brand signal a button system owns; three simultaneous primaries on billing screens (the highest-trust surfaces) reads as three products. The emerald fills also use a semantic success color decoratively as a CTA fill, directly violating foundation/color-hierarchy.ts's 90/8/2 rule.
- suggestion: Highest-leverage single fix: convert EstimateStatusActions, InvoiceStatusActions, InvoiceDetailActionBar, InvoicePaymentCollectionCard, and RecordPaymentForm to buttonClassName() variants (add a 'success'-tone variant decision consciously or drop it). ~10 files retire two of the three primary languages.

### [P2/SYSTEMIC] Status badge color language forked three ways; 'sent' means different colors on invoices vs estimates
- category: status-system | effort: MEDIUM
- evidence: System A: StatusPill (shared/design-system/components/StatusPill.tsx) — 12 importers, but among ~20 domain badge components only CustomerStatusBadge.tsx adapts it (the documented target pattern, see its comment). System B: shared/lib/operational-status-styles.ts duplicates StatusPill's five tone strings verbatim (lines 9-18) and feeds Job/Invoice/Estimate/Expense/Dispatch/Priority/TimeEntry/Membership badges. System C: LeadStatusBadge via shared/lib/leads/lead-status.ts:19-26 uses raw Tailwind palette (sky/violet/indigo/amber/emerald/slate — three non-semantic decorative hues) plus a hex-hardcoded north-star map at shared/components/leads/north-star-m14/lead-north-star-styles.ts:10-17 (#EFE4CB, #8A6324). Cross-entity inconsistency: operational-status-styles.ts:31 invoice sent=info vs :49 estimate sent=warning. Dispatch north-star maps hardcode rgba/hex (DispatchStatusBadge.tsx:11-23) — dark exception is sanctioned, hex is not.
- why: Users learn status colors as a vocabulary; leads speaking violet/indigo while every other entity speaks the semantic tone set breaks scanning, and the same word 'sent' switching from blue to amber between two document types undermines the meaning of both. The verbatim tone-string copy in operational-status-styles.ts is one refactor away from silent drift against StatusPill.
- suggestion: Make operational-status-styles.ts import StatusPill's tone map instead of copying it; rewrite LeadStatusBadge as a StatusPill adapter like CustomerStatusBadge; decide sent=info everywhere (or document the estimate exception in the tokens file).

### [P2/SYSTEMIC] Destructive confirmations split between AltairConfirmDialog and native window.confirm — sibling lifecycle controls diverge
- category: dialog-primitive | effort: LOW
- evidence: AltairConfirmDialog/AltairDialog: 8 importers (incl. shared/components/invoices/InvoiceLifecycleControl.tsx, expenses/ExpenseLifecycleControl.tsx). window.confirm: 11 files — CustomerLifecycleControl.tsx:63,107,156, CustomersBulkActionBar.tsx:65,83,101, JobLifecycleControl.tsx:58, JobsBulkActionBar.tsx:66, EstimateBatchSelectionBar.tsx:52, InvoiceBatchSelectionBar.tsx:52, others. So the invoice lifecycle control shows a branded dialog while the customer and job lifecycle controls — the same interaction pattern in the same entity-detail slot — throw an unstyled browser confirm.
- why: Native confirm() is unstylable, shows the raw origin URL, can't explain consequences, and is the exact place (deactivate/void/bulk operations) where user trust is decided. Two sibling controls behaving differently makes the polished one look accidental.
- suggestion: Port the 11 window.confirm call sites to AltairConfirmDialog; the lifecycle-control family is nearly identical code, so one adapter migration covers most of them.

### [P2/SYSTEMIC] Empty states exist in four visual families across sibling list pages
- category: primitive-adoption | effort: MEDIUM
- evidence: DS EmptyState (shared/design-system/components/EmptyState.tsx): 6 importers (Dispatch, Payments, Technicians, TimeClock, EstimatePipeline, MarketingAutomation). Twelve domain *EmptyState components split into families: (1) Customers/Jobs — token-based card, 9x9 icon tile (CustomersEmptyState.tsx:23-31, JobsEmptyState.tsx:66-75); (2) Invoices/Estimates — hex-hardcoded brass circle, 12x12 icon (InvoicesEmptyState.tsx:46-52, EstimatesEmptyState.tsx:46-52 — #EFE4CB, #8A6324, #17130E, and #64748B which is raw slate-500 hex); (3) Leads — CSS class 'admin-empty-state' + hex/slate mix (LeadsEmptyState.tsx:18-32); (4) DS EmptyState proper. Plus 47 ad hoc '>No …<'/'Nothing …' paragraphs in shared/components (e.g. dashboard/DashboardCompactSummaries.tsx:163,347,416,680).
- why: Empty states are the first thing every new tenant sees on every list page; four different icon sizes, radii, and palettes across adjacent nav items (Customers → Invoices → Leads) is the most visible consistency failure a trial user encounters, and two of the families hardcode hex against the project's own token rule.
- suggestion: Fold the 12 domain components into thin adapters over DS EmptyState (Dispatch already demonstrates the pattern in DispatchEmptyState.tsx); sweep the 47 inline paragraphs opportunistically.

### [P2/SYSTEMIC] Three copy-paste sibling families: 11 StatStrips, 10 SearchFilterBars, 6 MobileCardLists (~2,800 lines re-implementing 3 patterns)
- category: sibling-duplication | effort: MEDIUM
- evidence: StatStrips: 11 files, 935 lines (customers, dispatch, estimates, expenses, invoices, jobs, leads, payments, service-items, payroll, time-clock) — identical interactive label+value+filter-queue pattern with literally duplicated class constants (CustomersStatStrip.tsx:29-32 labelClass/valueClass == JobsStatStrip.tsx:58-60). SearchFilterBars: 10 files (customers, dispatch, estimates, expenses, invoices, jobs, leads, service-items, time-clock x2), each re-declaring its own northStar-vs-legacy searchInputClass ternary (EstimateSearchFilterBar.tsx:46, ExpenseSearchFilterBar.tsx:101, InvoiceSearchFilterBar.tsx:54). MobileCardLists: 6 files, 930 lines sharing adminListRowClass but repeating section/checkbox/chevron scaffolding (customers 94, estimates 189, expenses 212, invoices 204, jobs 145, payments 86 lines).
- why: Each family is one pattern expressed N times; every polish decision (row height, focus ring, filter-pill active state) must be made N times and currently isn't — which is exactly how the per-page inconsistencies in this audit accumulate. New panels (Schedule, Time Clock, Settings redesigns are next on the roadmap) will clone them again unless a primitive lands first.
- suggestion: Extract a GlanceStatStrip, a ListSearchFilterBar shell, and a MobileCardList row scaffold into shared/design-system before panels 14-16 are built; migrate domains incrementally.

### [P2/LOCAL] Technicians panel ships a third ledger structure instead of AltairTable; service-items and time-clock tables hand-roll headers
- category: table-primitive | effort: MEDIUM
- evidence: AltairTable adopted by 7 core ledgers (CustomersTable, EstimatesTable, ExpensesTable, InvoicesTable, JobsTable, LeadList, PaymentsTable). Technicians (active panel 13) instead renders a div/ul grid ledger with a bespoke 10px uppercase tracking-[0.12em] header and --north-star-plate-border var (shared/components/technicians/TechniciansPageView.tsx:112-122) — different header typography, row material, and no <table> semantics. Raw <table> with slate-hardcoded headers on reachable pages: shared/components/service-items/ServiceItemsTable.tsx:112 ('border-slate-100/90 bg-white text-xs … text-slate-500', price-book route) and time-clock/TimeEntriesTable.tsx:25; 13 raw-<table> files total.

- suggestion: Rebuild the Technicians roster on AltairTable (or, if the card-grid is intentional, document it as an exception in table/README.md); migrate ServiceItemsTable header material to AltairTableHeader.

### [P3/SYSTEMIC] Form field components have exactly one consumer; the class layer lives under two names
- category: form-primitive | effort: LOW
- evidence: Field/Input/Select/Textarea (shared/design-system/components/): sole importer is shared/components/customers/CustomerForm.tsx:9-16. All other big forms use raw elements + classes: fieldControlClass family in 39 files; its alias adminFormInputClass (defined shared/design-system/shell/tokens.ts:108 as '= fieldControlClass') in 19 files incl. JobForm.tsx:155ff, EstimateForm.tsx:198ff, InvoiceForm. 34 local '*inputClass' const re-declarations (e.g. ExpenseForm.tsx:31 'const inputClass = fieldControlClass'). 52 files contain raw <input|select|textarea> with no field-style import at all (search bars, upload boxes, auth, JobTechnicianAssignment).
- why: The visual layer is actually consolidated (one class), so risk is lower than it looks — but two import names for one class plus a component layer with one adopter means label/error/description markup (what Field owns: spacing, aria wiring) is still hand-rolled per form, and the DS components will bit-rot as a facade nobody uses.
- suggestion: Either commit to the Field/Input components (migrate JobForm next — it is already 100% adminFormInputClass so the change is mechanical) or delete them and bless field-styles as the contract; retire the adminFormInputClass alias in favor of the field-styles names.

### [P3/SYSTEMIC] Skeleton wrapper re-declared identically in 35 files; 24 of 49 LoadingStates bypass the Master loading primitives
- category: loading-primitive | effort: LOW
- evidence: grep 'function Skeleton|const Skeleton' = 35 files each re-declaring the same 3-line wrapper over the 'admin-skeleton' CSS class (e.g. shared/components/dashboard/OperationalDashboardLoadingState.tsx:14-16). MasterListPageLoadingState: 16 importers; MasterDetailPageLoadingState/MasterBillingDetailOverlayLoadingState: 12. 24 of 49 *LoadingState files import none of them — mostly known-legacy areas (reports, time-clock, settings, network) but also active Dashboard (OperationalDashboardLoadingState.tsx) and Dispatch.
- why: Pure mechanical duplication with a one-line fix; every re-declaration is a chance for the skeleton material to diverge from the shell's, and 49 loading files is the largest sibling family in the codebase.
- suggestion: Export a single <Skeleton> from shared/design-system/shell (it already owns the admin-skeleton material) and delete the 35 local copies.

### [P3/SYSTEMIC] No design-system layering scale; z-index is ad hoc up to z-[70]
- category: layering | effort: LOW
- evidence: Values in shared/components + app: z-10 x25, z-50 x13, z-20 x11, z-40 x7, z-30 x5, z-[60] x5, z-[1] x4, z-[70] x1 (shared/components/mobile/QuickNavToggle.tsx:28), z-[5], z-[2]. The only documented scale is a comment inside MobileSheet.tsx:28-32 ('60 … can stack above FocusedDocumentOverlay (z-50)'); AltairDialog hardcodes z-50 (dialog/AltairDialog.tsx:123); shared/design-system contains no z tokens (grep zIndex = 0 hits).
- why: Stacking currently works by folklore: the sheet knows about the overlay only via a comment, and the next overlay author has to grep to find the ceiling. This is how a dialog ends up under a drawer in a future change — low cost now, expensive to debug later.
- suggestion: Add a small layer scale (nav/overlay/sheet/toast) to shared/design-system/shell/tokens.ts and point AltairDialog, MobileSheet, MobileSideDrawer, FocusedDocumentOverlay, and QuickNavToggle at it.

### [P3/SYSTEMIC] Eyebrow micro-label typography hand-rolled 362 times with 12 different tracking values
- category: typography-duplication | effort: MEDIUM
- evidence: grep 'text-[10|11px] … uppercase tracking' in shared/components = 362 occurrences; tracking distribution: tracking-wide x151, [0.14em] x54, [0.12em] x48, [0.08em] x23, [0.16em] x20, widest x19, [0.18em] x13, [0.1em] x8, [0.22em] x7, [0.2em] x6, [0.13em] x5, wider x3. SectionHeader exists (26 importers) but the label style itself has no exported class; altairSectionTitleAccentClass has only 2 users.
- why: The uppercase eyebrow is Altair's most repeated typographic gesture — 12 letter-spacing flavors means side-by-side cards visibly disagree at the exact size range (10-11px) where tracking differences read as sloppiness.
- suggestion: Export one (or two: on-paper / on-canvas) eyebrow class from the design system and codemod the arbitrary tracking values toward it.
